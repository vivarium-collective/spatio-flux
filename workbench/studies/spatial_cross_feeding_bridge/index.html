Spatial cross-feeding bridge

Q Does a spatially-separated consumer (strain B) grow only where a producer (strain A) secretes a diffusing metabolite?

Claim

Strain A grows on glucose and secretes acetate that diffuses to strain B on the no-glucose side, producing a small, acetate-dependent growth increment in B that does not occur when A is absent — an emergent, spatially-localized cross-feeding dependency (the effect is modest: B grows ~1.6% over the run).

Background

Question Does strain B grow only where strain A's secreted acetate diffuses to it?

Model & approach

Model spatial_cross_feeding_bridgemodel →
Measures 3 behavior testssee Tests →

Result

Strain B grows only when strain A is present to secrete acetate: with A, B's biomass overlaps the diffused acetate field and increases modestly (~1.6%); with A absent, no acetate forms and B stays flat. The A-absent control is the decisive evidence that the small increment is A's acetate bridge, not residual glucose or incidental growth. The magnitude is small (acetate is a poor carbon source) — the claim is about the spatial dependency, not the yield.
